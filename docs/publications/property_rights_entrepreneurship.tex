\begin{abstract}
    This article examines the relationship between the quality of property rights institutions and entrepreneurial activity, using panel data from 90 countries between 2004 and 2018. The results provide evidence of a U-shaped association: at low levels of property rights enforcement, entrepreneurship rates are elevated because of necessity-driven activity; as institutions improve moderately, total entrepreneurship initially declines but, at higher levels of property rights protection, opportunity-driven entrepreneurship rises, increasing overall entrepreneurial activity.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Economic Freedom of the World Index, entrepreneurship, Global Entrepreneurship Monitor, property rights, rule of law

\noindent\textbf{JEL Classification:} L26, P14

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}
%% ============================================================

[Content of this section from published paper.]

The main specification is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
    \text{Entrepreneurial activity}_{it} = \beta_1 + \beta_2 \cdot \text{Property rights}_{it} + \beta_3 \cdot \text{Property rights}_{it}^{2} + \mathbf{X}_{it}\boldsymbol{\beta} + u_i + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{X}_{it}$ is the matrix of control variables and $u_i$ are random effects.

\subsection{Sample}\label{subsec:sample}
%% -----------------------------------

[Content of this section from published paper.]\footnote{See \url{https://www.gemconsortium.org/wiki/1149}.}\footnote{See \url{https://www.gemconsortium.org/wiki/1154}.}\footnote{See \url{https://www.gemconsortium.org/wiki/1177}.}\footnote{See \url{https://www.gemconsortium.org/wiki/1180}.}

The sample comprises 822 observations from 90 countries over the period 2004--2018, drawn from the Fraser Institute's Economic Freedom of the World (EFW) index and the Global Entrepreneurship Monitor (GEM).

%% -----------------------------------
\subsection{Dependent Variables}\label{subsec:dependent_variables}
%% -----------------------------------

[Content of this section from published paper.]

The dependent variable is decomposed into opportunity-driven and necessity-driven components, estimated separately:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:opportunity}
    \text{Opportunity}_{it} = \beta_1 + \beta_2 \cdot \text{Property rights}_{it} + \mathbf{X}_{it}\boldsymbol{\beta} + u_i + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessity}
    \text{Necessity}_{it} = \beta_1 + \beta_2 \cdot \text{Property rights}_{it} + \mathbf{X}_{it}\boldsymbol{\beta} + u_i + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}
